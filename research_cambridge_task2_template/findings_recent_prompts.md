# Recent Cambridge IELTS Task 2 Prompts: Template Reliability Check

Research date: 6 August 2026

## Scope and edition verification

The newest verifiable volume available by the research date is **IELTS 21 Practice Tests**, published in **July 2026**. Cambridge University Press & Assessment states that it contains four authentic papers, is available in both Academic and General Training editions, and was developed by the same team that creates the IELTS exam.

- Official Cambridge catalogue: https://www.cambridge.org/cambridgeenglish/catalog/cambridge-english-exams-ielts/ielts-21-practice-tests
- Official reliability: **Very high** for publication date, editions, authenticity, and four-test structure.
- Limitation: the official catalogue page does not expose all eight Task 2 prompts as text. Prompt wording below is therefore verified through secondary transcriptions, with the source strength stated per group.

## Prompt set

### 1. Academic Test 1: opinion / agree or disagree

> The best way to provide enough homes in large cities is to build tall apartment blocks.
>
> To what extent do you agree or disagree with this statement?

- Attribution: IELTS 21 Practice Tests, Academic, Test 1, Writing Task 2.
- Source reliability: **High confidence**. The wording matches two independent full-page transcriptions.
- Sources:
  - https://engnovate.com/ielts-writing-tests/cambridge-ielts-21-academic-writing-test-1/
  - https://practicepteonline.com/ielts-writing-test-129/
- Template test: two body paragraphs can provide two reasons supporting one clear degree of agreement. A partial-agreement answer is a special branch because the body paragraphs may support different sides.

### 2. Academic Test 2: discuss both views and give an opinion

> Some people say that in the digital age, theatres and cinemas are no longer important as people can watch all the entertainment they want online. Others argue that theatres and cinemas are still important both economically and culturally.
>
> Discuss both these views and give your own opinion.

- Attribution: IELTS 21 Practice Tests, Academic, Test 2, Writing Task 2.
- Source reliability: **High confidence**. The wording matches two independent full-page transcriptions.
- Sources:
  - https://engnovate.com/ielts-writing-tests/cambridge-ielts-21-academic-writing-test-2/
  - https://practicepteonline.com/ielts-writing-test-130/
- Template test: body paragraph 1 explains the online-entertainment view; body paragraph 2 explains the economic/cultural-importance view. The introduction and conclusion must state the writer's opinion, and the body should make that opinion visible rather than merely listing two views.

### 3. Academic Test 3: advantages outweigh disadvantages

> All university undergraduate courses should include a period of time spent studying abroad or doing a work placement.
>
> Do you think the advantages of this would outweigh the disadvantages?

- Attribution: IELTS 21 Practice Tests, Academic, Test 3, Writing Task 2.
- Source reliability: **High confidence**. The wording matches two independent full-page transcriptions.
- Sources:
  - https://engnovate.com/ielts-writing-tests/cambridge-ielts-21-academic-writing-test-3/
  - https://practicepteonline.com/ielts-writing-test-131/
- Template test: the two bodies can cover disadvantages and advantages, but the essay must compare their **relative weight**. A generic advantages/disadvantages conclusion is insufficient.

### 4. Academic Test 4: two questions, both evaluative

> Some people argue that primary schools focus too much on formal learning.
>
> To what extent do you agree with this opinion?
>
> How important do you think it is for children to play as well as learn in the primary school classroom?

- Attribution: IELTS 21 Practice Tests, Academic, Test 4, Writing Task 2.
- Source reliability: **High confidence**. The wording matches two independent full-page transcriptions.
- Sources:
  - https://engnovate.com/ielts-writing-tests/cambridge-ielts-21-academic-writing-test-4/
  - https://practicepteonline.com/ielts-writing-test-132/
- Template test: one body paragraph per question works, but this cannot be taught as a neutral two-question essay. The introduction must answer both the degree of agreement and the importance of play; the conclusion should repeat both judgments.

### 5. General Training Test 1: opinion / agree or disagree

> It is very common for people to change both their habits and their opinions as they get older.
>
> Do you agree or disagree?

- Attribution: IELTS 21 Practice Tests, General Training, Test 1, Writing Task 2.
- Source reliability: **Medium-high confidence**. The transcription is presented with an officially scored candidate response and detailed examiner comments, but only one independent public prompt transcription was located within the search budget.
- Source: https://ieltswritingchecker.org/cambridge-ielts-21-general-training-writing#test-1
- Template test: straightforward same-side reasoning; both body paragraphs should support a consistent position.

### 6. General Training Test 2: discuss both views and give an opinion

> Some people believe that international sports events, such as The Olympics or the Football World Cup, are a good way to bring countries together. Others say that these events create competition, not friendship, between countries.
>
> Discuss both these views and give your own opinion.

- Attribution: IELTS 21 Practice Tests, General Training, Test 2, Writing Task 2.
- Source reliability: **Medium-high confidence** for the same reason as General Training Test 1.
- Source: https://ieltswritingchecker.org/cambridge-ielts-21-general-training-writing#test-2
- Template test: confirms that the opposing-view branch is relevant to both Academic and General Training. Each view must be explained, and the writer's own position must be explicit.

### 7. General Training Test 3: two direct questions

> Nowadays many large international companies offer their staff the opportunity to spend a period of time living and working in another country.
>
> Why might this opportunity be good for employees?
>
> What advantages does this development have for companies?

- Attribution: IELTS 21 Practice Tests, General Training, Test 3, Writing Task 2.
- Source reliability: **Medium-high confidence** for the same reason as General Training Test 1.
- Source: https://ieltswritingchecker.org/cambridge-ielts-21-general-training-writing#test-3
- Template test: the four-paragraph structure works cleanly: body 1 answers the employee question and body 2 answers the company question. The two body paragraphs are parallel answers, not opposing sides.

### 8. General Training Test 4: causes and solutions

> Doctors tell us that we can get all the vitamins we need from having a healthy diet. Despite this advice, some people continue to spend large sums of money on vitamin pills.
>
> Why do you think some people spend a lot of money on vitamin pills?
>
> How could people be persuaded to improve their diet instead of buying vitamin pills?

- Attribution: IELTS 21 Practice Tests, General Training, Test 4, Writing Task 2.
- Source reliability: **Medium-high confidence** for the same reason as General Training Test 1.
- Source: https://ieltswritingchecker.org/cambridge-ielts-21-general-training-writing#test-4
- Template test: body 1 is a cause/explanation paragraph. Body 2 is a solution paragraph and needs a different internal logic: **actor -> action -> implementation -> immediate effect -> longer-term result**. A generic reason-example-result sequence is not sufficient for the solution body.

## Reliability verdict for the merged template

The merged template is **reliable as one macro-level four-paragraph framework**, but not as one identical sentence-by-sentence micro-template.

The eight newest prompts support the following stable structure:

1. Introduction: paraphrase the topic and answer every instruction.
2. Body 1: task unit A.
3. Body 2: task unit B.
4. Conclusion: summarise the answers and restate any required judgment.

The project should keep these differences visibly marked inside that one framework:

| Prompt instruction | Relationship between body paragraphs | Non-negotiable difference |
|---|---|---|
| Agree/disagree | Two supporting reasons, normally same direction | State the degree of agreement; partial agreement is a special branch. |
| Discuss both views + opinion | Two opposing views | Cover both views and give the writer's opinion. |
| Advantages outweigh disadvantages | Two opposing effects | Make an explicit comparison of importance/scale, not just a list. |
| Two direct questions | Two parallel answers in question order | Answer both questions in the introduction and conclusion where practical. |
| Cause + solution | Explanation followed by action | Use a purpose-built solution chain with an actor and implementation detail. |

### Main design implication

The correct teaching claim is:

> **One four-paragraph framework, with different task relationships and paragraph engines.**

It would be misleading to say that every body paragraph always follows exactly the same six-step formula. Academic Test 4 and General Training Test 4 are particularly useful stress tests: the first requires two separate evaluative judgments, while the second changes the internal logic from a cause paragraph to a solution paragraph.

## Research limitations

- Cambridge confirms the edition, authenticity, publication month, and Academic/General Training versions, but its catalogue page does not publish all prompts in accessible text.
- Academic wording was cross-checked on two independent secondary sites. General Training wording was verified on one detailed secondary page that includes official candidate scores and examiner comments.
- No pirated book PDF was used.
- The research used five focused web searches; direct follow-through to result pages was used for source verification.
